Visualization Architecture: Which Graphs Are Suitable for Campus Grievance Portals?

1. Line Plot (Timeline & Trend)

Why it fits: Grievance volumes are continuous time-series data. A dual-line plot allows administrators to correlate surges with academic milestones (e.g., examination weeks, hostel admissions) and verify if the grievance cell maintains adequate resolution velocity.

2. Grouped Bar Plot (Departmental Workload)

Why it fits: Discrete categorical comparison between academic departments (CSE, ECE, MECH, Civil) and campus units (Hostel, Infrastructure). Grouping "Received" alongside "Resolved" immediately exposes understaffed or lagging units.

3. Doughnut Chart (Category Proportions)

Why it fits: Visualizes the relative percentage distribution across grievance types (Curriculum, Facilities, Mess, Conduct) without cluttering. The center cutout provides high aesthetic appeal and fast reading.

4. Horizontal Bar Plot (Pipeline Funnel)

Why it fits: Grievances follow a linear progression (Submitted → Under Review → Assigned → In Progress → Resolved → Closed). Horizontal bars provide clear label legibility and highlight workflow bottlenecks.
